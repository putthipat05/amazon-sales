**My business recommendation**
Price and discounts may not always be the primary factors influencing customers' purchasing decisions. Product quality, category, and overall value may also play important roles.

For example, the water heater received the highest customer demand despite having a discount of only 28%, while the MR.BAND Portal USB had a much higher discount of 77%. 
This suggests that a larger discount does not necessarily guarantee higher customer demand. However, discounts can still encourage purchases for certain products. For instance, the Sony TV achieved strong customer demand with a 44% discount, indicating that price reductions may help attract customers who are more price-sensitive.

Based on these findings, I recommend adopting a differentiated discount strategy. Products that already demonstrate strong customer demand should rely less on frequent promotions to protect profit margins.
Meanwhile, products with lower demand could be included in bundle promotions with popular products to increase their exposure and encourage trial purchases. By monitoring customer feedback and subsequent demand, the company can evaluate whether these products have the potential to improve or should eventually be discontinued.

### 2.Which product categories have the strongest customer demand, and what characteristics explain their performance compared with low-performing categories?

**My business recommendation**
As shown in the analysis, four out of the top five product categories by customer demand belong to the electronics category. In contrast, the bottom five categories include household products and certain types of electronics. This difference may be explained by the fact that many top-performing electronic products serve everyday needs, making them more likely to attract consistent customer demand.

To improve the performance of lower-demand categories, I recommend introducing household product bundles at special prices. By combining complementary household items into a single package, the company can encourage customers to purchase multiple products in one transaction while increasing the exposure of slower-moving inventory. This strategy may also improve customers' perceived value by offering a more attractive combined price than purchasing each item separately.

The company should test different bundle combinations, monitor customer demand and customer feedback, and evaluate whether the additional sales justify the discounts offered. This would help the business reduce excess inventory while providing customers with greater value for their money.
